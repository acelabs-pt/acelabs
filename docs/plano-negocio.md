# Business Plan Draft: Process Automation for Real Estate (Portugal)

**Purpose:** working document for the partnership meeting. Not a finished plan - it is a set of positions and decisions to argue through together.

---

## 1. The pitch, in one sentence

We install automated operating systems inside small and mid-sized real estate businesses - lead handling, listing production, compliance, and management reporting - built fast with AI coding agents, delivered as fixed-price projects with a monthly support contract.

We are not selling "AI". We are selling recovered hours and closed deals.

---

## 2. Why real estate, and why now

Real estate agencies in Portugal share a very consistent profile:

- Revenue is directly tied to speed. A portal lead that gets a reply in 5 minutes converts several times better than one answered the next morning. Most agencies answer late.
- Operations run on WhatsApp, Excel, Outlook, and a CRM that nobody fully uses.
- Listings are produced by hand: photos, description, translations, portal upload, repeated per portal.
- They have real regulatory obligations (AML/branqueamento under the mediação regime, IMPIC obligations, RGPD) that they handle informally and nervously.
- Owners are commercially minded, decide fast, and pay for things that visibly increase closings.

That last point matters most. A franchise owner with 8 agents can sign a €7.000 project in one meeting. An industrial client would need six months and a procurement process.

**Segments, in order of attractiveness:**

1. Independent agencies and single franchise units (Remax / ERA / Century21 / KW / Zome offices, 5-25 agents) - fastest decision, clearest pain.
2. Property managers and arrendamento operators - recurring, boring, highly automatable work.
3. Small developers / promotores - unit inventory, reservations, CPCV pipeline, cost vs budget control.
4. Condominium administrators - high transaction volume, low margins, desperate for admin relief.

Start with 1. Prove it. Expand sideways.

## 3. What we actually sell

Do not sell "automation consulting". Sell three named products with fixed scope and price. This is the single most important decision in the meeting.

### Offer A - Lead Engine (entry product)
Portal and website leads are captured, parsed, matched against current stock, answered automatically within minutes in the right language, logged in the CRM, and pushed to the right agent with full context.

*Sold on:* response time and conversion rate. Easiest thing in the world to demo.
*Price:* €3.500 - 5.500 build.

### Offer B - Management Cockpit (the sticky product)
A live dashboard for the owner: pipeline by stage, conversion by lead source, agent productivity, average days on market, commission forecast, stock ageing. Pulled automatically from CRM, portals, and their spreadsheets.

*Sold on:* the owner finally seeing their own business. This is what makes them keep paying us.
*Price:* €4.500 - 8.000 build, plus retainer.

### Offer C - Compliance Pack (our differentiator)
KYC/AML document collection and checklists, record keeping, retention rules, audit trail, and the periodic reporting pack. Structured properly, with data validation, so that when someone comes asking, everything is there.

*Sold on:* fear, correctly. Nobody in this market sells this well.
*Price:* €5.000 - 9.000 build.

**Deliberately not offering yet:** listing generation from photos, contract drafting, developer cost control. Good ideas, keep them as phase 2. Three offers is already one too many for a two-person team.

**Recommendation for the meeting:** launch with A + B as a bundle. C is our long-term moat but needs one reference client before we can sell it credibly.

## 4. Business model and pricing

| Stage | What it is | Price | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnóstico | Process audit, automation map, ROI estimate | €900 - 1.500 | 1 week |
| Build | One or two workflows implemented | €3.500 - 8.000 | 3-6 weeks |
| Retainer | Monitoring, fixes, small changes, monthly review | €400 - 1.200 / month | ongoing |

Key rules:

- **The diagnostic is paid, and credited against the build.** It filters tyre-kickers and gets us paid to write the proposal.
- **Fixed price, not hourly.** We are selling an outcome. Hourly pricing punishes us for being fast, which is our entire advantage.
- **The retainer is the business.** Builds pay the bills, retainers build the asset. Target: every build converts to a retainer, no exceptions. Write it into the proposal as a bundle, not an upsell.
- **The client owns the code.** Say it upfront, in writing. It kills the lock-in objection and costs us nothing, because they cannot maintain it without us anyway.

**The real long game:** after 5-6 agencies, the same automations repeat. At that point we stop selling projects and start selling a product with a monthly fee. Services now fund the product later. Say this out loud in the meeting so both of you are building toward the same thing.

## 5. Why us, specifically

This needs an honest answer, because anyone can buy a Claude subscription.

- **Him:** delivery speed with agentic tooling, and he has already paid for the training.
- **Me:** ten-plus years of data engineering under actual regulatory scrutiny. SQL, large-scale pipelines, data quality, validation, reconciliation, regulatory reporting for a supervised bank. I know how to build something that survives an audit.

The combination is the pitch. Most people selling AI automation build demos that break in month two. We build things that are correct, validated, and documented, because one of us has spent years being held to that standard by supervisors. In a sector with AML and RGPD obligations, that is a commercial argument, not just a technical one.

That is also my argument for equal equity.

## 6. Go to market: first 90 days

**Days 1-30 - Build the proof**
- Pick one agency we already have a relationship with. Do the whole thing for €1.500-2.500, at a loss, in exchange for a written case study, hard numbers, and a reference call.
- Record a 4-minute screen demo of the Lead Engine. This is the sales asset. More important than a website.
- Build a target list of 60 agencies within an hour of Braga/Porto/Guimarães, with the owner's name on each line.

**Days 31-60 - First paid clients**
- Direct outreach: owner-to-owner. Not cold email blasts. Short message, one specific observation about their business, offer a 30-minute call.
- Offer 10 free 30-minute audits. Convert 3 to paid diagnostics.
- Ask the reference client for two introductions. Referral is the only channel that will really work in this market.

**Days 61-90 - First builds**
- Deliver 2 builds. Convert both to retainers.
- Write down every reusable component. From client 3 onward, margin comes from reuse, not from working harder.

**Realistic target for year one, both of us part-time:** 1 reference client, 6-8 paid diagnostics, 4-5 builds, 3-4 retainers.

## 8. Setup in Portugal

To verify with a contabilista certificado and, for the contracts, a lawyer. This is a starting point, not advice:

- **Structure:** Sociedade por Quotas (Lda), can be set up same-day via Empresa na Hora for roughly €360. Capital social can technically be minimal but something like €5.000 reads better to clients.
- **Alternative for month one:** both invoice as ENI / recibos verdes while we validate. Cheaper, faster, and reversible. Form the Lda once there is a second paying client.
- **Certified accountant is mandatory** for the Lda. Budget €120-180/month.
- **IVA at 23%**, quarterly filings.
- **RGPD:** we will be a subcontratante (processor) handling personal data of buyers, sellers, and tenants. We need a data processing agreement in every client contract. Non-negotiable, and a selling point when competitors have not thought about it.

## 9. Partnership terms: what we decide in the meeting

The technology is the easy part. Partnerships die on these:

1. **Equity.** Proposal: 50/50. Equal risk, complementary skills, and neither of us is replaceable.
2. **The €2.000 training.** Not equity. Treat it as a company expense to be reimbursed from the first revenue, or as a personal investment he simply made. Either is fine. Ambiguity is not.
3. **Roles.** Proposal: he leads delivery and tooling, I lead data architecture, quality, and compliance. Sales is shared, but one of us has to own the pipeline or it will not happen. Decide who.
4. **Time commitment.** Concrete hours per week from each of us, written down. Unequal effort is the number one killer of side-business partnerships.
5. **Vesting.** 4 years, 1-year cliff. If either of us walks after three months, they do not keep half the company. Do this while we still like each other.
6. **Exit and deadlock.** Who is gerente, how a disagreement is broken, what happens if one wants out. An acordo parassocial, written by a lawyer, before the first client.
7. **IP.** Everything built for the company belongs to the company, not to whoever wrote the prompt.

---

## 10. Risks, stated plainly

- **We are a two-person shop with day jobs.** Delivery capacity is the ceiling. Fixed scope and reusable components are the only defence.
- **Model dependency.** We are building on a tool controlled by someone else, whose pricing and capabilities can shift. Mitigate by owning the architecture and data layer, not just the prompts.
- **Real estate is price sensitive and slow to adopt.** Some franchise units are also mandated to use network tools. Qualify for this on the first call.
- **The AI automation space is getting crowded fast.** Our defence is the regulated-data discipline and local relationships, not the technology.
- **Sales, not building, is the bottleneck.** Both of us are technical. Neither of us has sold before. This is the real gap in the plan and we should name it in the meeting.
